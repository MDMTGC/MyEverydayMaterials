How Flea Collars Work

Traditional flea collars emit pesticides — primarily Tetrachlorvinphos (TCVP), an organophosphate, or pyrethroids like permethrin — in a continuous low-dose release designed to kill fleas on contact.

The Health Risks

  • Neurological Toxicity: TCVP is classified as a possible human carcinogen by the EPA and is linked to neurological damage with chronic low-level exposure. Children who pet treated animals are particularly at risk.
  • Permethrin Danger to Cats: Permethrin is acutely toxic to cats. A dog treated with a permethrin collar or topical can accidentally poison a cat sharing the same bed or through social grooming.
  • Skin Irritation: The collar contact point often causes localized fur loss, skin inflammation, and chemical burns in sensitive animals.

What You Can Do Right Now

  • Wash Hands After Touching: Always wash hands after handling a treated pet, especially before food contact or around children.
  • Separate Multi-Pet Households: Never use permethrin products on dogs if cats share the home.
  • Consult a Vet: Newer oral isoxazoline medications (under veterinary supervision) provide effective flea control with a more targeted mechanism.
