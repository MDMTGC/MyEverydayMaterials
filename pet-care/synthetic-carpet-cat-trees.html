What Cat Trees Are Made Of

Most cat trees consist of a particleboard or MDF (medium-density fiberboard) core wrapped in synthetic plush carpet (typically polyester or nylon) bonded with solvent-based spray adhesives.

The Health Risks

  • Formaldehyde from the Core: MDF and particleboard use urea-formaldehyde (UF) resins as binders. UF resin is one of the most significant indoor sources of formaldehyde, classified as a Group 1 human carcinogen by IARC.
  • VOCs from Adhesives: Solvent-based spray adhesives used to attach carpet to the frame release benzene, toluene, and xylene — all known neurotoxins — especially in the first weeks of use.
  • Carpet Fiber Ingestion: Cats groom continuously. Shed synthetic carpet fibers from scratching posts are frequently ingested, potentially accumulating in the digestive tract.

What You Can Do Right Now

  • Off-gas Before Use: Unbox new cat trees and leave them outdoors or in a garage for 48–72 hours before bringing indoors.
  • Ventilate: Place the tree in a well-ventilated room, not in a small bedroom or bathroom where the cat sleeps.
  • Better Alternatives: Look for cat trees built from solid kiln-dried wood with natural sisal rope scratching posts and water-based adhesives. Certifications like CARB Phase 2 indicate lower formaldehyde emissions from board cores.
