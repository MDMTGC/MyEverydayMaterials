The Problem with Melamine Plastic

Many affordable pet bowls are made from melamine-formaldehyde resin — a hard plastic that looks like ceramic but degrades with use.

The Health Risks

  • Leaching into Food: When the surface is scratched (inevitable from pet teeth and dishwashers), melamine and formaldehyde can migrate into wet food or water, especially at elevated temperatures.
  • Feline Acne: Porous scratched surfaces trap Staphylococcus bacteria, a leading cause of painful feline chin acne.
  • Kidney Toxicity: Melamine is associated with kidney damage — the same compound implicated in the 2007 pet food contamination crisis.

What You Can Do Right Now

  • Inspect Weekly: Any bowl that feels rough or looks cloudy has exceeded its safe lifespan — recycle it.
  • Daily Washing: Wash bowls after every meal to prevent biofilm buildup regardless of material.
  • Upgrade: 304-grade stainless steel is non-porous, dishwasher safe, and impossible for bacteria to colonize.
