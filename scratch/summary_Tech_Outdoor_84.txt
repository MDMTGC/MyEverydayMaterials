The U.S. Geological Survey (USGS) provides extensive scientific information regarding rare earth elements (REEs) through its official website and research programs[1][2].

### Key USGS Resources on Rare Earth Elements
*   **General Overview:** The USGS defines rare earth elements as a group of 15 elements ranging from atomic number 57 (lanthanum) to 71 (lutetium), often including yttrium (atomic number 39) due to its similar chemical properties[1][3]. While these elements are not actually "rare" in terms of crustal abundance, concentrated, economically viable deposits are limited[1].
*   **Applications and Importance:** USGS documentation highlights the critical role of REEs in high-technology, defense, energy, and industrial applications[1][2]. They are essential for magnets (e.g., in wind turbines and electric vehicle motors), batteries, catalysts, and various defense systems[2][3][4].
*   **Mineralogy and Geology:** Information is available regarding the diverse mineralogy of REEs (e.g., carbonatites, ion-adsorption clays, and monazite-bearing deposits) and the geological processes that control their mineralization[1][2][5].
*   **Critical Minerals Research:** The USGS investigates domestic sources of critical minerals through initiatives like the **Earth Mapping Resources Initiative (EarthMRI)**[2]. This includes efforts to inventory, research, and assess domestic mineral potential to reduce reliance on foreign supply chains[2][3].
*   **Scientific Reports:** The USGS publishes various Scientific Investigations Reports and data products, such as the **USMIN Mineral Deposit Database**, which provides authoritative information on significant mines and mineral deposits in the United States[2][3].

You can explore these topics further by visiting the [USGS website](https://www.usgs.gov) and searching for "Rare Earth Elements" or "Critical Minerals" to access their latest data, interactive maps, and scientific publications[1][2].
