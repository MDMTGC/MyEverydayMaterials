The National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) provides comprehensive guidance on lithium-ion battery safety to help prevent fires and injuries[1][2]. Because these batteries store a large amount of energy in a small space, they can pose significant fire risks—including overheating, toxic gas release, and explosions—if they are damaged, defective, or used improperly[1][3][4][5].

### **Key Safety Recommendations**

*   **Purchase Certified Products:** Only buy and use devices, batteries, and charging equipment that have been listed or labeled by a nationally recognized testing laboratory[1][3][6].
*   **Use Proper Equipment:** Always use the specific battery and charging cord that came with your device[1][3][4][7]. Avoid using mismatched or cheap, uncertified third-party replacements[4][7][6].
*   **Follow Charging Best Practices:**
    *   Do not charge devices on soft surfaces like beds, couches, or under pillows, as these can trap heat[3][4][6].
    *   Place devices on hard, flat, non-flammable surfaces while charging[4][6].
    *   Stop charging once the battery is full; avoid leaving devices plugged in indefinitely or overnight[1][4][7].
*   **Storage and Handling:**
    *   Keep batteries at room temperature[3]. Avoid placing them in direct sunlight or hot vehicles[3].
    *   Store batteries and devices away from combustible materials[3][7].
    *   Never charge e-bikes or other large devices in hallways or near exits, as a fire could block your escape route[4][7].

### **Signs of a Problem**
Stop using a battery immediately if you notice any of the following warning signs[3][4]:
*   Odor or unusual smells[3][4]
*   Change in color[3]
*   Excessive heat[3][4]
*   Change in shape (swelling or bulging)[3][4]
*   Leaking or odd noises (e.g., hissing)[3][4]
