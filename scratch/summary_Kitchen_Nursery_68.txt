The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) provides guidance and information regarding melamine tableware in a resource titled **"Melamine in Tableware Questions and Answers"**[1][2].

Key points from the FDA's guidance include:

*   **Safety and Usage:** The FDA considers the risk of melamine migrating from tableware into food to be very low under normal conditions[3]. Melamine-formaldehyde resin is used to manufacture various products, including plates, cups, and cooking utensils[1].
*   **Microwave Warning:** The FDA explicitly advises against using melamine-based dinnerware in microwave ovens[1][4]. High temperatures can cause melamine to leach into food[1][5]. You should only use ceramic or other cookware specifically labeled as "microwave-safe" for heating food[1].
*   **Health Risks:** While migration is minimal in most cases, exposure to high levels of melamine can be harmful[1][3]. Contamination above FDA-assessed levels may be linked to health issues such as kidney stones, kidney failure, and other toxic effects[1][4][5].
*   **Product Care:** Because damaged surfaces can potentially release more chemicals, it is generally recommended to replace melamine tableware that has become scratched, cracked, or discolored[4][5].

For more detailed information, you can view the full document directly on the official FDA website by searching for "Melamine in Tableware Questions and Answers" on fda.gov[1][2].
