To ensure your safety when using, storing, and disposing of lithium-ion batteries, please follow these essential guidelines based on fire safety expert recommendations:

### **Charging Safety**
*   **Use Proper Equipment:** Always use the charging cable and power adapter that came with your device[1][2][3]. If you must replace them, use only manufacturer-approved, certified equipment[1][2][4].
*   **Charge on Hard Surfaces:** Charge devices on flat, hard, non-flammable surfaces (like a countertop or tile floor)[2][5][3]. **Never** charge on soft surfaces like beds, couches, or under pillows, as these can trap heat and create a fire hazard[2][3][6][7].
*   **Monitor Charging:** Avoid leaving devices unattended while charging, and do not charge them overnight or while you are sleeping[2][3][4]. Unplug the device once it is fully charged[1][2][3].

### **Storage & Handling**
*   **Room Temperature:** Store batteries and devices in a cool, dry place at room temperature[2][3][7]. Avoid extreme heat or cold[1][2][8].
*   **Keep Away from Hazards:** Do not store batteries near flammable materials, in direct sunlight, or in hot vehicles[1][2][3][9].
*   **Protect from Damage:** Avoid dropping, puncturing, or crushing batteries[10][3][11]. Regularly inspect your batteries for signs of damage, such as swelling, bulging, unusual odors, cracking, or hissing sounds[1][2][3]. If a battery is damaged, stop using it immediately[1][6][4].

### **Proper Disposal**
*   **Never Use Regular Trash:** **Do not** place lithium-ion batteries in household garbage or recycling bins[1][5][12]. They can ignite and cause fires in garbage trucks and landfills[1][4][13].
*   **Recycle Responsibly:** Take used or damaged batteries to a dedicated battery recycling center or a household hazardous waste collection point[1][4][12].
*   **Prepare for Disposal:** To prevent short circuits during transport
