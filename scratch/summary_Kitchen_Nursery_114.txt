The Ecology Center, through its Healthy Stuff Lab, has conducted periodic research since 2006 regarding toxic chemicals—specifically flame retardants (FRs) and per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS)—in children's car seats[1][2][3].

### Key Findings from Ecology Center Reports
*   **Persistent Presence of Chemicals:** While some manufacturers have made progress in reducing or eliminating certain hazardous chemicals, many car seats sold in the U.S. still contain flame retardants or PFAS[4][2]. In their 2022 report, the Ecology Center found that 55% of tested U.S. car seats contained flame retardant chemicals in major components[4][5].
*   **Shift in Flame Retardants:** As manufacturers move away from certain well-known toxic flame retardants, they have often replaced them with phosphorus-based flame retardants, the toxicity profiles of which are often unknown[4][2].
*   **PFAS Use:** Some car seats and strollers utilize PFAS for water and stain resistance[4][2]. Testing has identified these chemicals in a portion of products, and the Ecology Center urges companies to eliminate them[4][2].
*   **Impact of Flammability Standards:** The Ecology Center emphasizes that these chemicals are often added to meet outdated U.S. federal flammability standards[1][2][5]. They advocate for updating these standards, noting that European car seats often contain fewer flame retardants while still meeting safety requirements[2][5].
*   **Market Progress:** There has been a positive trend in the availability of car seats marketed as FR-free[4][6][5]. As of their 2022 findings, over 40 car seat models from multiple companies were available without added flame retardants[4][2].
