The European Chemicals Agency (ECHA) has conducted significant work regarding **Aromatic Brominated Flame Retardants (ABFRs)**, culminating in an investigation report published in December 2024[1][2][3].

Key information regarding ECHA's activities on this topic includes:

*   **Investigation Report (December 2024):** ECHA submitted an investigation report to the European Commission that highlighted significant environmental risks associated with ABFRs[1][2][4]. The report concluded that non-polymeric additive ABFRs pose a particularly high burden on the environment[1][5][6].
*   **Key Findings:**
    *   **Hazards:** Several ABFRs were identified as persistent, bioaccumulative, and toxic (PBT) or very persistent and very bioaccumulative (vPvB)[1][2][7].
    *   **Non-Polymeric Additives:** These substances are of greatest concern because they are not covalently bound to the polymer matrix, making them more prone to leaching into the environment[1][7][5].
    *   **Regulatory Status:** ECHA noted that many of these substances are not registered under REACH, raising concerns about compliance and the composition of imported articles[3][5].
*   **Regulatory Mandate:** Following the investigation, the European Commission gave ECHA a mandate to:
    1.  **Prepare a restriction dossier** on non-polymeric additive ABFRs under the REACH Regulation[2][4].
    2.  **Conduct an investigation report** on organophosphorus flame retardants (OPFRs) and other flame retardants to avoid "regrettable substitution" (where hazardous substances are replaced by equally problematic alternatives)[2][4][6].
