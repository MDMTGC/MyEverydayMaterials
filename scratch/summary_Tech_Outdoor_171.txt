The U.S. Geological Survey (USGS) provides comprehensive statistics, geological information, and market analysis for rare earth elements (REEs)[1][2].

### **Overview of Rare Earth Elements**
Rare earth elements consist of 17 elements: the 15 lanthanides (atomic numbers 57–71), plus scandium (atomic number 21) and yttrium (atomic number 39)[1][3]. Despite their name, they are relatively abundant in the Earth's crust, but minable concentrations are less common than for many other mineral commodities[4][5][3].

### **Key Resources for USGS Data**
*   **Mineral Commodity Summaries (MCS):** The USGS publishes an annual *Mineral Commodity Summary* report that provides the most up-to-date statistics on domestic production, imports, exports, consumption, and global market trends[4][6].
    *   [Access the latest Rare Earths report via USGS](https://www.usgs.gov) (search for "Mineral Commodity Summaries Rare Earths").
*   **Historical Statistics (Data Series 140):** The USGS maintains long-term historical data, including production, trade, and consumption figures dating back as far as 1900 for various commodities[2].
*   **Earth Mapping Resources Initiative (Earth MRI):** This USGS program focuses on acquiring data to better understand domestic sources of critical minerals, including rare earths[1].

*   **End Uses:** Leading domestic end uses include catalysts, batteries, permanent magnets, ceramics/glass, and metallurgical applications[4][5][8]. Globally, permanent magnets are a primary application[4].
