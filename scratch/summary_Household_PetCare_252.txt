Formaldehyde is a colorless, pungent-smelling gas that is common in indoor environments[1][2][3]. Because it is a volatile organic compound (VOC), it can easily be released into the air from various household products and materials through a process known as "off-gassing"[1][4][5][6].

### **Common Sources of Formaldehyde in the Home**
Formaldehyde is widely used in manufacturing and is found in many everyday items[1][7][3]. Significant sources include:
*   **Pressed-Wood Products:** These are often the most significant indoor sources[2][7]. They include particleboard (used in cabinetry, furniture, and sub-flooring), hardwood plywood paneling, and medium-density fiberboard (MDF)[2].
*   **Combustion Sources:** Burning materials such as tobacco, wood, natural gas, kerosene, or gasoline produces formaldehyde[4][8][9]. This includes use of gas stoves, un-vented kerosene heaters, and fireplaces[2][7].
*   **Household & Personal Care Products:** It can be found in paints, coatings, glues, adhesives, permanent-press fabrics (draperies and clothing), cosmetics, and some cleaning products[8][2][10][9].

### **Health Effects**
Exposure to formaldehyde can cause various health issues, with the severity often depending on the concentration and duration of exposure[1][5].
*   **Common Symptoms:** At elevated levels (typically above 0.1 ppm), it can cause watery eyes, burning sensations in the eyes, nose, and throat, nausea, and difficulty breathing[2][3]. 
*   **Respiratory Issues:** It may trigger asthma attacks or worsen respiratory conditions[8][2][9].
*   **Long-Term Risks:** Formaldehyde is classified as a carcinogen[11][10][9]. Long-term exposure is linked to certain types of cancer, such as nasopharyngeal cancer[8][10][9].
