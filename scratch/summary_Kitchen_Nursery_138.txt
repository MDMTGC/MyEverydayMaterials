Phthalates are a group of chemicals often used as plasticizers to increase the flexibility, durability, and longevity of plastics and other materials[1][2][3]. Due to potential health concerns, including endocrine disruption and developmental impacts, their use is strictly regulated, particularly in children's products[1][2][4].

### **CPSC Phthalate Regulations**
The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) regulates phthalates in children’s toys and child care articles under the Consumer Product Safety Improvement Act (CPSIA)[1][4].

*   **Prohibition:** It is illegal to manufacture for sale, distribute, or import into the United States any children's toys or child care articles that contain more than **0.1% (1000 ppm)** of the following phthalates in any accessible plasticized component[1]:
    *   Di-(2-ethylhexyl) phthalate (**DEHP**)
    *   Dibutyl phthalate (**DBP**)
    *   Benzyl butyl phthalate (**BBP**)
    *   Diisononyl phthalate (**DINP**)
    *   Diisobutyl phthalate (**DIBP**)
    *   Di-n-pentyl phthalate (**DPENP**)
    *   Di-n-hexyl phthalate (**DHEXP**)
    *   Dicyclohexyl phthalate (**DCHP**)

*   **Definitions:**
    *   **Children’s Toy:** A consumer product designed or intended by the manufacturer for a child 12 years of age or younger for use when the child plays[1].
    *   **Child Care Article:** A product designed or intended by the manufacturer to facilitate sleep or the feeding of children age 3 and younger, or to help such children with sucking or teething[1].

### **Business Guidance & Compliance**
*   **Certification:** Manufacturers and importers of children's products subject to these regulations must certify compliance by issuing a **Children's Product Certificate (CPC)**[1]. The citation to use in section 2 of the CPC is "16 C.F.R. part 1307 – Phthalates"[1].
*   **Testing Exemptions:** While the CPSC has determined that certain materials (such as specific unfinished engineered wood, untreated fibers, and certain plastics) are exempt from testing requirements, **this does not exempt firms from the underlying phthalate requirements**[1]. Firms are still responsible for ensuring their products meet the 0.1% limit and for certifying compliance[1].
*   **Accessibility:** Guidance on how to determine if a component is "accessible" (and thus subject to the regulation) can be found in **16 C.F.R. part 1199**[1].

For more detailed information, businesses should visit the official [CPSC Business Guidance page](https://www.cpsc.gov) to stay updated on regulatory requirements and compliance procedures[1].
