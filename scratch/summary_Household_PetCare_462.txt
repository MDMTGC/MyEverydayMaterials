Silent Spring Institute provides extensive research and resources regarding **phthalates**, a class of chemicals widely used as plasticizers to increase the flexibility and durability of plastics, as well as in personal care products and fragrances[1][2][3][4].

### Key Insights from Silent Spring Institute Research
*   **Endocrine Disruption:** Silent Spring identifies phthalates as endocrine-disrupting chemicals (EDCs) that can mimic or interfere with the body's hormonal systems[3][4]. These disruptions are associated with various health concerns, including reproductive and developmental issues, fertility challenges, cancer, asthma, and metabolic disorders[5][3][6][4].
*   **Consumer Exposure:** Research from the Institute highlights that people are widely exposed to phthalates through various everyday sources, including[3][4]:
    *   **Personal Care Products:** Phthalates are often hidden in ingredients like "fragrance" in lotions, shampoos, and cosmetics[7][2][4].
    *   **Household Products:** They are found in vinyl flooring, shower curtains, mattress covers, and other household items[3][4].
    *   **Food Packaging:** Phthalates can leach into food from packaging materials[3][4].
*   **Health Disparities:** The Institute’s **POWER Project** and other studies have investigated how different populations are disproportionately exposed to these chemicals[5][8]. For example, research indicates that Black women often have higher levels of phthalates in their bodies, which may be linked to the use of specific personal care products such as hair smoothing products and skin lightening creams, as well as societal factors[5][4].
