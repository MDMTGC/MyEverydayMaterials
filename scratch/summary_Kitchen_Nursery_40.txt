Contact dermatitis in children is a skin reaction that occurs when a child's skin comes into contact with a substance that causes either irritation or an allergic reaction[1][2][3].

### Types of Contact Dermatitis
*   **Irritant Contact Dermatitis:** This is the most common form[2][3]. It happens when a substance directly damages the skin's outer layer[1][2]. Common irritants for children include soaps, detergents, saliva, urine (often causing diaper rash), baby lotions, perfumes, and certain foods[3].
*   **Allergic Contact Dermatitis:** This occurs when the immune system has an allergic reaction to a specific substance (allergen)[2][3]. Common allergens include certain metals (like nickel, often found in jewelry, belt buckles, and zippers), fragrances, and plants like poison ivy, poison oak, and sumac[4][3].

### Symptoms
Symptoms can vary by child but often include[1][3]:
*   Redness and swelling[1][3]
*   Itching (which can be intense)[1][3]
*   Blistering, oozing, or crusting[1][5][3]
*   Dry, cracked, or peeling skin[1][3]
*   Pain or tenderness[1]

In children, common areas affected include the face, neck, hands, legs, and the diaper area[5].

### Management and Treatment
*   **Identify and Avoid:** The most important step is to identify the irritant or allergen and prevent further contact[1][4].
*   **Cleanse the Skin:** Wash the affected area with mild soap and water as soon as possible after suspected exposure[1][3].
*   **Soothe the Skin:** Applying cool, wet compresses can help reduce inflammation and itching[1][3].
*   **Medications:** Over-the-counter or prescription corticosteroid creams may be used to reduce inflammation[1][3]. Oral antihistamines may help relieve itching[1][3].

### When to See a Doctor
If the reaction is severe, does not improve with basic care, or if you are unsure of the cause, it is important to consult a pediatrician or a dermatologist[4][3]. A doctor can help confirm the diagnosis, sometimes using patch testing, and provide a tailored treatment plan[4].
