The European Chemicals Agency (ECHA) published its **Regulatory Strategy for Flame Retardants** on March 15, 2023[1][2][3]. This high-level strategy was developed to assess flame retardants as a group rather than individually, aiming to minimize the use of hazardous substances and prevent "regrettable substitution," where banned chemicals are replaced by other potentially harmful alternatives[4][5][3][6].

### Key Aspects of the Strategy:
*   **Purpose:** The strategy outlines a comprehensive, science-based approach to regulate flame retardants, acknowledging their wide use in consumer goods (e.g., electronics, furniture, building materials) while addressing their persistent, bioaccumulative, and toxic (PBT) properties[4][1][7][8].
*   **Aromatic Brominated Flame Retardants (ABFRs):** The strategy identified these as candidates for EU-wide restriction due to concerns about their persistence and potential toxicity[1][5][8]. ECHA has since conducted investigation work—including an investigation report delivered in December 2024—to support the European Commission in deciding whether to mandate a formal restriction dossier[4][7].
*   **Other Flame Retardant Groups:**
    *   **Aliphatic brominated and organophosphorus flame retardants:** The strategy noted that more data is needed for these groups[9][5]. ECHA planned to reassess these in 2025 as new data became available[9][10].
    *   **Non-halogenated flame retardants:** For certain non-halogenated subgroups, no immediate regulatory action was identified as they showed low or unlikely hazards at the time of the strategy's publication[9][5].
