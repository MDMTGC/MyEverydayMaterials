The **AOEC Exposure Code List** (often referred to as the AOEC Asthmagen Database) is a resource maintained by the Association of Occupational and Environmental Clinics (AOEC) to help clinicians and researchers identify potential occupational and environmental causes of asthma[1][2][3].

### Accessing the Database
You can access the **AOEC Exposure Codes Look-Up** tool directly to search for specific substances, exposure codes, or to view the full list of asthmagens[2][4]:

*   **AOEC Exposure Code Lookup:** [https://www.aoecdata.org/](https://www.aoecdata.org/) (or via the [MSU OEM](https://msu.edu) portal)[2][4]

### Key Information About the List
*   **Purpose:** The list is designed to assist in identifying existing and emerging occupational and environmental health concerns[1][3]. It is regularly updated (typically twice a year) based on systematic reviews of peer-reviewed literature conducted in collaboration with researchers at Michigan State University[2][3].
*   **Asthmagen Designations:** Substances on the list are categorized based on whether they meet specific criteria for asthmagens[1][5]:
    *   **Rs:** Reviewed and meeting criteria for **sensitizer-induced asthma**[5][4].
    *   **Rr:** Reviewed and meeting criteria for **irritant-induced asthma** (Reactive Airways Dysfunction Syndrome, or RADS)[5][4].
    *   **Rrs:** Reviewed and meeting both criteria[5].
    *   **G:** Generally accepted as asthmagens[1][5].
    *   **R-:** Reviewed but does not meet the specific AOEC criteria[1][5].
*   **Important Limitations:**
    *   The list is **not exhaustive**[1]. Many substances have not been formally evaluated, and others not yet designated as asthmagens may still be capable of inducing asthma[1].
    *   The designations are tools for clinical guidance and are not official governmental regulations[1]. Clinicians are encouraged to use their independent judgment when evaluating patient exposures[1].
