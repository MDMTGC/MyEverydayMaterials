Why is there Teflon in Floss?

To create a "smooth-glide" experience between tight teeth, many major brands coat their floss with **Polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE)**, the same chemical used in non-stick cookware.

The Health Risks (The Deep Dive)

  • Blood Serum Levels: A study published in the Journal of Exposure Science & Environmental Epidemiology found that women who used a specific brand of glide-style floss had higher levels of PFAS (specifically PFHxS) in their blood.
  • Oral Absorption: Because the gums are highly vascularized, there is a direct pathway for chemical migration from the floss coating into the bloodstream during daily use.

What You Can Do Right Now

  • The Snap Test: If your floss is flat, ribbon-like, and glides effortlessly without shredding, it is likely coated with PTFE.
  • Switch to Natural: Look for flosses made from **Silk** or **Organic Cotton** coated in beeswax or carnauba wax, which are naturally PFAS-free.
